The 5-transistor OTA, mechanically

Jargon decoder for the stage-3 spec — µCox, gm/ro, GBW, process corners, phase margin. For the interactive circuit these terms belong to, see 5t-ota-circuit.html in the same folder.

The jargon you flagged

µCox — the factory constant

ID = ½ · µCox · (W/L) · Vov²

µ = how fast carriers drift through the channel (silicon property). Cox = gate-oxide capacitance per area = εox/tox — how strongly the gate's field couples into the channel.

Their product sets how much current you get per unit of (W/L) and Vov². You never choose it — it comes from the PDK's model file. It is also exactly what varies between chips, which is where the corners below come from.

Vov, gm, ro — the operating point

Vov = VGS − Vth   gm = 2ID/Vov   ro = 1/(λID)

Vov is how far past turn-on the gate sits. Pick it small → more gm per current (good gain, speed) but less headroom; pick it large → the opposite.

gm = how many amps of steering you get per volt of input. ro = how good a current source the transistor is. Gain = gm·(ro∥ro4) — and in this symmetric approximation that works out to 1/(λ·Vov): gain is set by L and Vov, not by current. Current is what sets speed:

GBW — the speed spec

An op-amp's gain is not one number: it is 40 dB at low frequency, then falls at −20 dB/decade. GBW is the frequency where the gain crosses 0 dB — the single number summarising "how fast".

GBW = gm / (2π·CL)

For the 5T OTA it is set by the input pair's gm and the load capacitance. Spec: ≥ 1 MHz into 10 pF → forces gm ≥ 63 µS → forces the 13 µA tail current. This chain is the stage-3 hand calculation.

ss / ff / sf — the process corners

Plot every wafer as a point: NMOS speed on one axis, PMOS on the other. The extremes of the distribution form the corners of the square — that's the name's origin.

ss = slow-slow: higher Vth, lower µ → less current for the same W/L → your GBW sags. ff = fast-fast: too much current → phase margin erodes. sf/fs: mismatch between the two device types — worst case for offset.

The PDK ships a SPICE model file per corner. You re-run the same testbench five times; the spec must hold in all five. (Corner shifts are typically tens of mV on Vth and 10–20% on µ — orders, not exact numbers.)

Phase margin — why 60°

Wire the op-amp's output back toward its input (feedback) and the loop can turn stable negative feedback into oscillation if the signal arrives late enough. Phase margin measures the safety distance from that edge; 60° is the standard target.

Capacitance anywhere in the signal path adds delay — which is why load caps, layout parasitics (PEX), and the stage-4 Miller compensation keep re-appearing in this ladder.

Everything here is the square-law idealisation (gain exactly 1/(λ·Vov) = 100, GBW = 1.03 MHz — right at spec, no margin: in stage 3 you'd keep headroom). Real sky130 devices deviate, and seeing how they deviate is what stages 1–3 teach.
